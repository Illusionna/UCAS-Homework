§ I

The Statistic & Its Pedigree

When the population variance σ² is unknown — which it almost always is — the standardised mean of a sample of size n no longer follows the Gaussian distribution law. Student, writing in 1908 under a pseudonym imposed by his employer (the Guinness Brewery), worked out the exact distribution of this awkward quantity, and so handed the world its first practical small-sample inferential tool.

The t-statistic replaces σ with the sample standard deviation s; the price is heavier tails, controlled by a single integer parameter ν — the degrees of freedom. As ν → ∞ the distribution converges, gracefully, to the standard normal distribution.

What follows unpacks this elegant little distribution — its shape, its tails, and the inference that it quietly powers more than a century later.

$$t \;=\; \dfrac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}} \;\sim\; t_\nu, \qquad \nu = n - 1 \qquad\qquad (1)$$

— the t-density, built from the Gamma function —
Regularised · Incomplete · Beta
(3-4)
$$\beta(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\mathrm{d}t=\displaystyle\dfrac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)},\quad I(x;a,b)=\dfrac{\beta(x;a,b)}{\beta(a,b)}$$
— Euler's Beta and its regularised cousin on $[0,1]$, with the symmetry $I(x;a,b)=1-I(1-x;b,a)$ —
★ the bridge to t distribution
(5)
$$F(t;\nu)=\Pr(X\leqslant t)=1-\dfrac{1}{2}I\left(\dfrac{\nu}{\nu+t^2};\dfrac{\nu}{2},\dfrac{1}{2}\right),\qquad t>0$$
— the t-CDF reduces to one call to the regularised incomplete beta —
Lentz continued fraction
(6)
$$I(x;a,b)=\dfrac{x^a(1-x)^b}{a\beta(a,b)}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1+\dfrac{d_1}{1+\dfrac{d_2}{1+\dfrac{d_3}{1+\dots}}}}$$
$d_{2m}=\dfrac{m(b-m)x}{(a+2m-1)(a+2m)}$     |     $d_{2m+1}=-\dfrac{(a+m)(a+b+m)x}{(a+2m)(a+2m+1)}$
inverse CDF · Newton-Raphson method
(7)
$$x_{n+1}=x_n-\dfrac{\text{CDF}(x_n;\nu)-p}{\text{PDF}(x_n;\nu)}$$
— iterate to recover any quantile $t_{\alpha,\nu}$ tabulated below —

From PDF to table. Density (2) is symmetric & bell-shaped, with tails heavier than the normal. No closed-form antiderivative exists.

Equation (5) reduces the integral to one call to $I(x;a,b)$, evaluated by the Lentz continued fraction (6).

Inverting via Newton-Raphson method (7) recovers any quantile $t_{\alpha,\nu}$ — every entry tabulated below.
